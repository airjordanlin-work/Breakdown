## Key points
- The cross step is a toprock step: one foot steps across in front of the other, then back out.
- Stay bouncy and relaxed. The step rides on a small bounce, not on stiff legs.
- Arms swing loose and opposite to the stepping leg so the upper body looks easy.
- Chest stays up and the head stays level. The legs do the traveling.

## Common mistakes
- Stiff, locked arms that hang or flap instead of swinging loose.
- Standing too tall and straight-legged, so there is no bounce.
- Stepping across without turning the hips with the step, so the cross looks flat.
- Short, timid steps that never really cross over.

## Fixes
- Soften the knees and let the bounce come first, then add the step.
- Let the arms swing and stay loose. A relaxed elbow reads cleaner than a locked one.
- Let the hips turn with the step, then back, like a gentle twist.
- Commit to the cross. Reach the foot all the way over before it comes back.

## Cue wording
- Short and warm, one idea at a time: "Soften that knee", "Loosen up", "Cross it all the way".
- Praise the feel when a rep is clean with plain words like "Nice and loose".
- Never promise the dancer is improving. Say what this attempt showed.

- When nothing was measured as off, lead with the feel: loose, bouncy, committed.
